Will Metro Manila Adults Act on the Alert?

Our study tests whether the way an emergency alert is written changes how well people understand it and whether they would act on it. This site is a prototype of the system we'd use: a simulator that runs the survey, a composer that writes alerts, and a checker that finds missing parts.

Five parts of an alert: hazard, location, time, guidance (what to do), and source. Each version is written for four alert types: flood, extreme heat, earthquake, and air quality.

What a respondent goes through

  1. Consent and a quick 18+ and Metro Manila check
  2. Gets one version (A to D), assigned in balanced blocks
  3. Sees four alerts on a lock screen; each hides after OK or 30 seconds
  4. Answers a comprehension check and how likely they'd act, after each alert
  5. Picks which version of the flood alert they'd want to receive

Design notes

The alert is hidden before the questions so people answer from memory, like after a real alert.

Answer options are shuffled so position doesn't hint at the right one. For typical alerts, the correct answer to "what to do" is that the alert didn't say.

An attention check appears after the second alert. Failed responses are flagged and left out of the results.
